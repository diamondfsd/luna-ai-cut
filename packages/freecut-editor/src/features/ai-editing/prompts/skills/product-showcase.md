# Product Showcase

## Creative judgment

- Establish the intended viewer, viewing context, product promise, proof and desired takeaway before designing the sequence.
- Establish what the product is and why the viewer should care as early as the available evidence allows. A strong visible result can lead before explanation when the material supports it.
- Let real product imagery carry the story. Use text to clarify a promise or transition, not to replace missing visual proof.
- For a substantial creation, form a concise shot plan before the EditProgram. Give each shot one purpose, one visual focus and a clear relationship to the shots around it.
- Choose shot count, duration, rhythm, and structure from the available material and the user's intent. A hook, development, proof, payoff and ending are useful narrative functions, not mandatory template slots.
- Treat a single still as one source that can support deliberate reframing. Do not describe it as multiple real screens or shots.
- Use changes in shot size, motion and stillness to establish hierarchy. Avoid decorative motion or transitions that do not express a product idea.

## Evidence and safety

- Inspect the current project and the eligible image or video assets before making editorial claims.
- Request visual analysis for assets with no description. Never infer interface content or product features from filenames.
- Decide from the conversation and current timeline whether the user wants a new standalone piece, an extension, or a revision. Preserve relevant existing work.

## Quality standard

- The result should have an intentional visual hierarchy, readable on-screen text, continuous visible coverage, intelligible sound where present, and an ending that feels deliberate.
- Re-read the workspace returned by the applied EditProgram. Check the actual source ranges, framing, motion, captions, track reuse, transitions, warnings and coverage before calling the result finished.
